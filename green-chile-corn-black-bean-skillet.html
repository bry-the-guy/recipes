Green Chile, Corn & Black Bean Skillet

A smoky Tex-Mex rice skillet with corn, two cans of black beans, mild green chiles, and melted cheddar.

5–6 servings · about 40 minutes, including 10 minutes prep (estimated)

Recipe supplied by Bryan Engle, developed with Claude.

Ingredients

  • 15 ml olive oil
  • 1/2 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 30 g tomato paste
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 5 g smoked paprika
  • 3 g ground cumin
  • 2 g garlic powder
  • 1 g onion powder
  • 1 g dried oregano
  • 1 g chili powder, optional
  • 185 g uncooked long-grain white rice
  • 600 ml low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1 can diced tomatoes (about 400 g)
  • 1 can mild diced green chiles, with juice
  • 2 cans black beans (about 240 g drained each), rinsed
  • 300 g corn, frozen or drained canned
  • 110 g shredded cheddar cheese
  • 2 green onions, chopped, for serving
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped, for serving
  • 1 avocado, chopped, optional

Directions

  1. Heat the olive oil in a large deep skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and lightly browned, about 6 minutes.
  2. Add the tomato paste, garlic, smoked paprika, cumin, garlic powder, onion powder, oregano and optional chili powder. Cook for about 1 minute until fragrant and the onion is coated.
  3. Add the rice, low-sodium vegetable broth, diced tomatoes and green chiles with their juice. Stir well, cover and simmer gently for 15–18 minutes, or until the rice is tender.
  4. Stir in the rinsed black beans, corn and half the cheddar. Cook for another 2–3 minutes, or until everything is hot; frozen corn may need a little longer. Taste before adding salt, since the canned ingredients and cheese already contribute some.
  5. Sprinkle the remaining cheddar over the top. Cover until melted, or place under a hot broiler for 2–3 minutes if using an oven-safe skillet.
  6. Top with green onions, coriander and optional avocado before serving.

Notes

  • Makes approximately 5–6 servings. Use a large, deep skillet with a lid.
  • For more cheese coverage, add an optional extra 40–50 g cheddar on top.
  • If the skillet is getting full, stir the beans and corn in a little at a time.
  • Green chile can size was not specified in the supplied recipe. Nutrition estimates assume a small 127 g can.
  • If the rice is still firm, continue cooking covered; add a splash of water if the pan is drying out. If it is tender but too wet, simmer uncovered briefly before adding the cheese topping.
