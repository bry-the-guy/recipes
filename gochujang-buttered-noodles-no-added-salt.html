Gochujang Buttered Noodles, Lighter and More Filling

Same garlicky, sweet-spicy sauce, with more protein and veg and less butter, pasta and honey.

4 servings (assumed, one egg per serving)

Ingredients

  • 4 large eggs
  • 300 g whole wheat spaghetti
  • 300 g broccoli florets
  • 200 g frozen shelled edamame
  • 40 g unsalted butter
  • 12 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 73 g gochujang paste (not sauce)
  • 30 ml honey
  • 59 ml rice vinegar or sherry vinegar
  • 200 g baby spinach
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 4 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds

Directions

  1. Bring a large pot of unsalted water to a boil. Lower in the eggs and cook for 6 minutes 30 seconds, then move them to a bowl of cold water. Keep the water boiling for the pasta.
  2. Add the spaghetti to the same pot and cook according to the package. For the last 3 minutes, add the broccoli florets and frozen shelled edamame. Scoop out about 250 ml of the cooking water, then drain everything and return it to the pot.
  3. While the pasta cooks, melt about two thirds of the butter in a skillet over medium-low heat. Add the finely chopped garlic and cook, stirring now and then, until the garlic softens but doesn't brown, about 2 minutes 30 seconds.
  4. Stir in the gochujang paste, honey and vinegar. Turn the heat to medium-high and simmer, stirring constantly, until thick enough that a spatula dragged across the pan leaves a trail for a few seconds, about 3 minutes 30 seconds. Take off the heat.
  5. Pour the sauce into the pot with the pasta and vegetables. Add the rest of the butter and the baby spinach, and stir vigorously until the butter melts and the spinach wilts. Loosen with splashes of the reserved cooking water. Season with black pepper. Taste the finished dish before deciding whether it needs any salt.
  6. Peel and halve the eggs. Divide the noodles into bowls and top with eggs, sliced scallions and toasted sesame seeds. Serve right away.

Notes

  • No added salt is needed by default; gochujang already provides saltiness. Use unsalted butter and unsalted cooking water.
  • Swap the eggs for 400 g pan-fried firm tofu, or 300 g shrimp seared in the skillet before the garlic.
  • For a milder portion, pull out some plain buttered noodles and vegetables before the sauce goes in.
  • If the sauce tastes too sharp from the vinegar, stir in an extra teaspoon of honey.
